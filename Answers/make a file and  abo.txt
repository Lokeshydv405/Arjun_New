**Section B: Short Answer Questions (20 marks)**

1. What are the common properties of metals? (4 marks)
Answer: Metals have several common properties, such as high density, malleability, ductility, high melting and boiling points, and the ability to conduct electricity and heat.

2. What are the differences between metals and nonmetals? (4 marks)
Answer: Metals are typically hard and shiny, while nonmetals are generally soft and dull. Metals are often highly reactive, while nonmetals are less reactive. Metals tend to be good conductors of electricity, while nonmetals are poor conductors.

3. What are the uses of metals and nonmetals? (4 marks)
Answer: Metals have a wide range of applications, including construction, transportation, electronics, and medicine. Nonmetals, on the other hand, are used in various industries like the production of fertilizers, fuels, and semiconductors.

4. What is the difference between a metal and a metalloid? (4 marks)
Answer: Metalloids are elements that exhibit some properties of metals, such as being malleable, but also have properties of nonmetals, like being poor conductors of electricity. Examples of metalloids include silicon and germanium.

5. What are some of the uses of industrial metals like copper and steel? (4 marks)
Answer: Copper is widely used in electrical wiring and circuits, while steel is used in construction and manufacturing.

**Section C: Long Answer Questions (20 marks)**

1. Describe the chemical reactions involved in the extraction of metals from their ores. (8 marks)
Answer: Metals are typically extracted from their ores through a process involving chemical reactions, such as electrolysis, smelting, or solvent extraction. These reactions involve the conversion of the metal ore into its pure form, often through the use of heat, chemicals, and electrical energy.

2. Explain the concept of alloys and give examples of different metal alloys. (6 marks)
Answer: An alloy is a mixture of two or more metals that have been combined to produce a material with unique properties. Alloys can have improved strength, durability, or conductivity compared to the individual metals, making them useful in various applications. Examples of metal alloys include brass (copper and zinc), steel (iron and carbon), and bronze (copper and tin).
